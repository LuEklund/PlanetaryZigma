## Particles — is there a smarter way?

Our system is stateless and analytic: each particle is a closed-form function of (emitter, index, age), computed in the vertex shader. It has zero CPU cost and zero per-particle memory, is deterministic, and hot-reloads.
- Literature (UFPE paper, Auckland lecture): stateless is the standard choice when particles don't need to react to a changing world.
- The only thing it can't do is collide with moving objects or keep history. That needs stateful compute particles: buffers updated every frame, and ping-pong or atomics to spawn and kill.
- **Recommendation: stay stateless.** Get the "nicer" from richer closed forms instead.
- Added this session: exponential drag `v(1−e^{−kt})/k`, gravity `−½gt²`, smooth color gradients, an HDR glow multiplier that feeds bloom, and shapes (spark streak along its own velocity, expanding shockwave ring). Plus a ring placement for telegraphs.
- Move to compute particles only if we need sparks bouncing off terrain, or attractors.
- Next cheap wins:
  - Soft particles: fade near geometry using depth. That needs the depth image sampled in its own pass (we'd copy depth or split the pass).
  - Flipbook-free procedural smoke: noise inside the fragment shader.
  - Ribbon trails for bullets: line placement between the last two positions, kept alive.

## AA

FXAA is now part of the post chain (decision 0015).
- MSAA would look sharper on our low-poly edges, but needs a multisampled color and depth pair, a resolve pass, and every pipeline rebuilt with a sample count.
- TAA ghosts on fast particles and needs motion vectors.
- SMAA needs lookup textures we can't generate (asset rule).
- Suggestion: try MSAA ×4 only if FXAA looks too soft on your monitor.

## Telegraphs (helping players dodge)

- Built as a `telegraph` server event (position, radius) and a `ring` particle effect: a fixed edge ring plus an inner ring that grows to the edge as the hit lands.
- Used by the hunkloid leap (landing spot), the bomber fuse (blast radius) and the charger dash (end point).
- A true projected decal would hug slopes perfectly, but needs depth sampling in a separate pass. The ring sits 0.3 m above the surface point and is fine on gentle terrain.
